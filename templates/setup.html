Vinyl Catalog · First-Run Setup

Step 1 of 3 Welcome

Welcome to Vinyl Catalog

An app for tracking your record collection — cover art, track listings, and live pricing data pulled from Discogs, with your own shelf positions (optional, once your collection grows) and notes. Everything stays on your Mac, stored in a local SQLite database.

What you will need:

✓ A free Discogs account — enables automatic album lookup by barcode or title

✓ Terminal open and running python3 app.py (you already did this — that's how you're seeing this page!)

Starting your collection

Add records one at a time by scanning a barcode, searching Discogs, or typing manually. Click ↺ Re-Sort at any time to sort your collection by artist last name, then by release year, then by title. A–Z alphabet divider markers can be added as a one-time setup step once your collection is underway.

Connect to Discogs

A free Discogs API token lets the app auto-fill artist, year, tracklist, and cover art when you scan a barcode or search by title.

How to get your free API token:

  1. Create a free account at discogs.com
  2. Go to Settings → Developers
  3. Click Generate new token
  4. Copy and paste it into the field below

Access Your Catalog

Your catalog runs locally on your Mac. Here is how to reach it:

On this Mac

http://localhost:5001

On your phone (same Wi-Fi)

http://{{ local_ip }}:5001

This is your actual network address — the wizard detected it automatically, so the URL above is correct for your setup. Type it into Safari on your iPhone while connected to the same Wi-Fi as your Mac and bookmark it for quick access. Your Mac's IP can occasionally change, so if it stops working, check the Terminal output next time you start the app and update your bookmark.

Keep Terminal open while using the catalog — closing it stops the app. To restart later, open Terminal and run these two commands:

cd ~/Desktop/music-catalog
python3 app.py

Your first shelf

All records start unsorted. Add a few, then click ↺ Re-Sort in the top bar to arrange them A–Z. Use Shelf Plan once your collection is big enough to span multiple physical shelves.
